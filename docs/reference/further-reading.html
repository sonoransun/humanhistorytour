Further Reading

Where to go next, chapter by chapter. This is a curated starting point — a shelf you could actually read — not an exhaustive scholarly bibliography. Every title has been checked against publisher and library catalogue records for its author, exact title and year; the selection leans deliberately global, giving Africa, the Americas, Asia beyond China, and Oceania real weight alongside Europe.

Big-picture histories

A handful of single works try to hold the whole span at once — 300,000 years, or 13.8 billion, in one argument. They are the best way to see the tour's through-lines as one story rather than nine. Read them together and against each other: the sweeping syntheses are exhilarating precisely because they simplify, so a couple carry real caveats worth keeping in mind.

How the dates work. Each year is the book's first publication. A translated book shows its original year and its English year; for textbooks and much-revised works the edition to read is named; where a book was retitled between Britain and America, both titles are given.

Sapiens: A Brief History of Humankind

Yuval Noah Harari · 2011 (English 2014)

The source of the tour's "Cognitive Revolution" framing. Harari's own spine has three revolutions — cognitive, agricultural and scientific; the industrial and information turns are this tour's extension of it — and the book is a dazzling synthesis of how shared fictions let strangers cooperate. Caveat: specialists flag sweeping generalizations and contested claims; treat it as a provocative framework, not settled fact.

Guns, Germs, and Steel: The Fates of Human Societies

Jared Diamond · 1997

Argues that geography and biogeography — not any innate difference between peoples — shaped which societies grew powerful. Hugely influential and admirably anti-racist in intent. Caveat: widely critiqued for geographic determinism, and for underplaying culture, institutions, and human agency.

The History of the World

J. M. Roberts & Odd Arne Westad · 6th ed., 2013

The standard one-volume reference: comprehensive, judicious, and — in Westad's revisions — far more genuinely global than the older Euro-centred surveys it replaced.

Maps of Time: An Introduction to Big History

David Christian · 2004

The founding text of "Big History," placing human events inside 13.8 billion years of cosmic, geological, and biological evolution — the widest possible frame for the whole tour.

Why the West Rules — For Now

Ian Morris · 2010

Builds a numerical index of "social development" and tracks East and West across 15,000 years. Materialist and data-driven, it deliberately refuses any story of permanent Western superiority.

The Silk Roads: A New History of the World

Peter Frankopan · 2015

Re-centres world history on Central Asia and the connective tissue between civilizations — the best popular corrective to a Europe-first narrative, and a non-Eurocentric spine for the tour.

Energy and Civilization: A History

Vaclav Smil · 2017

Every society is bounded by the energy it can harness. Smil's rigorous, numerate account is the deep background for the tour's energy through-line — from muscle and fire to fossil fuels and beyond.

The Earth Transformed: An Untold History

Peter Frankopan · 2023

The whole human story retold through climate and environment — droughts, volcanoes, monsoons, disease and deforestation — on every continent. The long-run companion to the tour's climate spine.

Era by era

Five to eight books for each of the nine chapters. Where a chapter's story is usually told from Europe, the picks here deliberately widen the lens — to Australia's deep past, the peopling of the Americas, the Islamic golden age, West African empires, Cahokia, Polynesia, the steppe, and the East and South Asian worlds.

I · Deep Prehistory & Human Origins ~7 Mya – 300 kya · Chapter I

Chris Stringer · 2011 (US ed. 2012)

Lone Survivors: How We Came to Be the Only Humans on Earth (first published in Britain as The Origin of Our Species) — a classic popular account of modern human origins from a long-time champion of the recent-African-origin model, written before Jebel Irhoud (2017) pushed Homo sapiens back to ~300,000 years; Stringer has since argued for a more complex, pan-African picture he calls "African multiregionalism". Pair it with Paul Pettitt's Homo Sapiens Rediscovered: The Scientific Revolution Rewriting Our Origins (2022) for the update.

Richard Wrangham · 2009

Catching Fire: How Cooking Made Us Human — argues that the control of fire and cooking, not raw meat, drove the human gut to shrink and the brain to grow.

Rebecca Wragg Sykes · 2020

Kindred: Neanderthal Life, Love, Death and Art — a vivid, up-to-the-minute portrait of our closest cousins, drawing on the last two decades of discoveries.

Yuval Noah Harari · 2011 (English 2014)

Sapiens (opening chapters) — its account of the once-crowded human family bush and the "cognitive" leap frames the first two chapters of the tour.

Svante Pääbo · 2014

Neanderthal Man: In Search of Lost Genomes — the first-person account of how a Neanderthal genome was actually sequenced, by the man who won the 2022 Nobel for doing it. The best available answer to "how could anyone possibly know that?"

Tom Higham · 2021

The World Before Us — a dating specialist on how radiocarbon, ancient DNA and palaeoproteomics rebuilt the timeline of the other human species, Denisovans included.

Lee Berger & John Hawks · 2017

Almost Human: The Astonishing Tale of Homo naledi and the Discovery That Changed Our Human Story — the Rising Star excavation, a small-brained hominin alive alongside early Homo sapiens, and the still-contested claim that it buried its dead.

II · Paleolithic & the Cognitive Revolution 300 kya – 12,000 BCE · Chapter II

David Reich · 2018

Who We Are and How We Got Here: Ancient DNA and the New Science of the Human Past — how the ancient-DNA revolution rewrote the story of human migration and admixture across every continent.

Billy Griffiths · 2018

Deep Time Dreaming: Uncovering Ancient Australia — the archaeology of the first Australians, whose continuous occupation of Sahul runs at least 50,000 years deep — and, on the contested Madjedbebe dates, perhaps 65,000.

David Lewis-Williams · 2002

The Mind in the Cave: Consciousness and the Origins of Art — a leading (if debated) interpretation of Ice-Age cave art and the arrival of symbolic minds.

Brian Fagan · 2010

Cro-Magnon: How the Ice Age Gave Birth to the First Modern Humans — the peopling of glacial Europe and the long overlap with the Neanderthals.

Jennifer Raff · 2022

Origin: A Genetic History of the Americas — a geneticist on how and when people reached the Americas from Beringia, weighing ancient genomes against the archaeology and taking Indigenous perspectives, and the ethics of sampling, seriously.

III · The Neolithic Revolution 12,000 – 3500 BCE · Chapter III

James C. Scott · 2017

Against the Grain: A Deep History of the Earliest States — grain, taxation, and coercion at the roots of the first states; a bracing counter to farming-as-pure-progress.

David Graeber & David Wengrow · 2021

The Dawn of Everything: A New History of Humanity — uses global archaeology to dismantle the tidy line from agriculture to inequality to the state.

Steven Mithen · 2003

After the Ice: A Global Human History 20,000–5000 BC — a genuinely worldwide tour of the postglacial transition, from the Americas to Africa to East Asia.

Brian Fagan · 2004

The Long Summer: How Climate Changed Civilization — how the unusually stable Holocene climate made settled farming, and everything after it, possible.

Peter Bellwood · 2005

First Farmers: The Origins of Agricultural Societies — farming's independent beginnings compared across the Fertile Crescent, China, New Guinea, Africa and the Americas, with the strong (and still debated) thesis that farming often spread with farmers and their languages.

Christopher Ehret · 2023

Ancient Africa: A Global History, to 300 CE — Africa from the end of the last Ice Age to its first kingdoms, rebuilt from archaeology and the evidence of languages: independent advances from ceramics and cotton weaving to iron smelting, crops exported to other continents, and the deeply African roots of ancient Egypt.

IV · First Civilizations 3500 – 1200 BCE · Chapter IV

Eric H. Cline · 2014

1177 B.C.: The Year Civilization Collapsed — the deeply interconnected Late Bronze Age Mediterranean and Near East, and the cascading systems collapse that ended it.

Amanda H. Podany · 2022

Weavers, Scribes, and Kings: A New History of the Ancient Near East — a current, humane synthesis that puts ordinary people alongside the kings of Mesopotamia and its neighbours.

Toby Wilkinson · 2010

The Rise and Fall of Ancient Egypt — three thousand years on the Nile told as a readable, unromantic narrative of power.

Andrew Robinson · 2015

The Indus: Lost Civilizations — the vast, orderly cities and still-undeciphered script of the Harappan world — the era's great South Asian civilization, often left out of the story.

Laura Spinney · 2025

Proto: How One Ancient Language Went Global — the Indo-European puzzle, from steppe herders meeting farmers around the Black Sea some 5,000 years ago to languages spoken from Bengal to Wales, retold from linguistics, archaeology and ancient DNA.

V · Classical Antiquity 1200 BCE – 500 CE · Chapter V

Mary Beard · 2015

SPQR: A History of Ancient Rome — the current standard popular history of Rome: skeptical about the myths, attentive to slaves, women, and the unfree majority.

Mark Edward Lewis · 2007

The Early Chinese Empires: Qin and Han — the eastern counterweight to Rome: a comparably vast empire forged at the same historical moment, half a world away.

Peter Brown · 1971

The World of Late Antiquity, AD 150–750 — the book that recast the "fall" of Rome as a long cultural transformation, and opened up the whole field of late antiquity.

Josephine Quinn · 2024

How the World Made the West — argues that "Western civilization" was always a product of contact and exchange across Africa and Asia, not a pure Greco-Roman line of descent.

Romila Thapar · 2002

Early India: From the Origins to AD 1300 — the classic one-volume history of early South Asia, from the Harappans through the Mauryas and Guptas to the southern kingdoms and the Indian Ocean trade, alert to how its sources were made.

VI · The Medieval World 500 – 1450 CE · Chapter VI

Janet L. Abu-Lughod · 1989

Before European Hegemony: The World System A.D. 1250–1350 — the classic demonstration of a thriving, polycentric Afro-Eurasian economy centuries before Europe's rise.

Michael A. Gomez · 2018

African Dominion: A New History of Empire in Early and Medieval West Africa — Ghana, Mali, and Songhay as the great imperial powers they were, gold, scholarship, and all.

Jim Al-Khalili · 2010 (US ed. 2011)

The House of Wisdom: How Arabic Science Saved Ancient Knowledge and Gave Us the Renaissance (first published in Britain as Pathfinders: The Golden Age of Arabic Science) — a physicist's tour of the Abbasid golden age, arguing that scholars writing in Arabic did original science rather than merely preserving Greek learning, when Baghdad and Córdoba, not Paris, were the centres of learning.

Charles C. Mann · 2005

1491: New Revelations of the Americas Before Columbus (in Britain: Ancient Americans: Rewriting the History of the New World) — the scale, sophistication, and sheer population of the pre-Columbian Americas, from the Andes to the Amazon.

Peter Frankopan · 2015

The Silk Roads (medieval chapters) — the Mongol peace and the Central-Asian trade world at the connective heart of the age.

Timothy R. Pauketat · 2009

Cahokia: Ancient America's Great City on the Mississippi — the largest pre-Columbian city north of Mexico, which boomed suddenly around 1050 CE, told by an archaeologist who has excavated there for decades.

Christina Thompson · 2019

Sea People: The Puzzle of Polynesia (UK subtitle: In Search of the Ancient Navigators of the Pacific) — how Polynesians found and settled the most remote islands on Earth; how outsiders from Cook onward tried to explain it; and how the double canoe Hōkūleʻa, sailed from Hawaiʻi to Tahiti without instruments in 1976, showed it could be done on purpose. The readable companion to Kirch below.

Marie Favereau · 2021

The Horde: How the Mongols Changed the World — the Golden Horde as a sophisticated, commercially minded steppe polity that took tribute from the Rus' principalities and linked the Mediterranean to Central Asia for more than two centuries.

VII · Early Modern Age 1450 – 1750 CE · Chapter VII

Charles C. Mann · 2011

1493: Uncovering the New World Columbus Created — the Columbian Exchange and the sudden birth of a single, globalized biology and economy.

Kenneth Pomeranz · 2000

The Great Divergence: China, Europe, and the Making of the Modern World Economy — the foundational case that Europe and China parted ways surprisingly late, around 1800, and largely because of accessible coal and the land and labour of the Americas.

John K. Thornton · 2nd ed., 1998

Africa and Africans in the Making of the Atlantic World, 1400–1800 — restores African agency to the Atlantic system rather than treating the continent as a passive victim. (The 1992 first edition, subtitled 1400–1680, stopped earlier.)

Timothy Brook · 2008

Vermeer's Hat: The Seventeenth Century and the Dawn of the Global World — global trade read through the objects in Dutch paintings, with China always in the frame.

Camilla Townsend · 2019

Fifth Sun: A New History of the Aztecs — the Mexica told from the Nahuatl-language annals their own descendants wrote, before, during and after the Spanish conquest.

Terence N. D'Altroy · 2002

The Incas — the Inka empire, Tawantinsuyu, from its rise in the 1400s to the Spanish invasion: roads, storehouses, khipu accounting and labour tax. (2nd ed. 2014.)

Toby Green · 2019

A Fistful of Shells: West Africa from the Rise of the Slave Trade to the Age of Revolution — West African states, currencies and credit from the 1400s, and how the unequal exchange of cowries and cloth for captives entrenched long-run inequality.

Pekka Hämäläinen · 2022

Indigenous Continent: The Epic Contest for North America — turns the usual story around: for most of four centuries it was Native powers — Haudenosaunee, Comanche, Lakota and others — that set the terms in North America, not European colonists.

VIII · Industrial & Modern Age 1750 – 1945 CE · Chapter VIII

E. P. Thompson · 1963

The Making of the English Working Class — the landmark "history from below" of what industrialization felt like to the people it remade.

Sven Beckert · 2014

Empire of Cotton: A Global History — industrialization as a worldwide system built on slavery, colonial land, and coerced labour, not just clever machines in Manchester.

Mike Davis · 2001

Late Victorian Holocausts: El Niño Famines and the Making of the Third World — how global markets and empire turned droughts into mass death in India, China, and Brazil.

Robert J. Gordon · 2016

The Rise and Fall of American Growth: The U.S. Standard of Living since the Civil War — argues the burst of innovation from 1870 to 1970 was a unique, perhaps unrepeatable century of transformation.

Jürgen Osterhammel · 2009 (English 2014)

The Transformation of the World: A Global History of the Nineteenth Century — the century as one connected world of migration, cities, frontiers, energy and empire; the global reference work for this chapter.

Laurent Dubois · 2004

Avengers of the New World: The Story of the Haitian Revolution — from the sugar plantations of Saint-Domingue to independence in 1804: the only slave revolt in history to found a lasting independent state.

IX · Information Age 1945 – present · Chapter IX

Walter Isaacson · 2014

The Innovators: How a Group of Hackers, Geniuses, and Geeks Created the Digital Revolution — the collaborative history of computing, from Ada Lovelace to the modern internet.

Odd Arne Westad · 2017

The Cold War: A World History — the era's defining conflict told as a genuinely global story, shaped as much by decolonization in Asia and Africa as by Washington and Moscow.

Kai-Fu Lee · 2018

AI Superpowers: China, Silicon Valley, and the New World Order — the artificial- intelligence landscape from someone at its US and Chinese centres at once — a rare non-US-only view.

Shoshana Zuboff · 2019

The Age of Surveillance Capitalism: The Fight for a Human Future at the New Frontier of Power — a critical anatomy of the data economy and the platform business models that now shape everyday life.

J. R. McNeill & Peter Engelke · 2016

The Great Acceleration: An Environmental History of the Anthropocene since 1945 — the post-war surge in energy use, population, cities and emissions as environmental history; read it alongside The Great Acceleration page.

Frederick Cooper · 2002 (2nd ed. 2019)

Africa since 1940: The Past of the Present — decolonization and its aftermath as one continuous story, and why new African states inherited the "gatekeeper" structures of the colonial ones.

David Van Reybrouck · 2020 (English 2024)

Revolusi: Indonesia and the Birth of the Modern World — Indonesia's independence struggle, built on interviews with people who lived it, as a hinge of the decolonizing world, the 1955 Bandung Conference included.

By theme & by method

The era-by-era list above follows the tour's spine. These shelves cut across it — the books that explain how any of this is known, the forces that ran underneath every chapter at once, the half of humanity and the unfree millions that general histories still under-tell, and the regions they routinely skip.

Method, evidence & how history gets written

Colin Renfrew & Paul Bahn · 1991; latest ed. 2024 (with Elizabeth DeMarrais)

Archaeology: Theories, Methods and Practice — the standard textbook, and the single best place to understand what a stratigraphic section, a calibration curve or an isotope ratio actually tells you. See How We Know for the short version.

E. H. Carr · 1961

What Is History? — still the sharpest short argument that facts do not select themselves, and that every narrative is an argument about what mattered.

Michel-Rolph Trouillot · 1995

Silencing the Past: Power and the Production of History — how silences enter the record at four moments, from the making of a source to the making of a narrative. Its central case is the Haitian Revolution, which most nineteenth-century histories simply declined to see.

David Reich · 2018

Who We Are and How We Got Here — also listed under Chapter II, but read here as a methods book: what ancient DNA can settle, what it cannot, and how "pots not people" finally became a testable question.

Climate, disease & the forces under every chapter

William H. McNeill · 1976

Plagues and Peoples — the book that put epidemic disease at the centre of world history rather than in its footnotes. Dated in places; foundational anyway.

Kyle Harper · 2021

Plagues upon the Earth: Disease and the Course of Human History — the long view of infectious disease: why farming, cities and trade kept breeding new pathogens, and why the last two centuries broke the pattern. The current successor to McNeill.

Frank M. Snowden · 2019

Epidemics and Society: From the Black Death to the Present — plague, smallpox, cholera, tuberculosis, malaria, HIV and Ebola, each read for how it reshaped states, medicine and ordinary lives.

Kyle Harper · 2017

The Fate of Rome: Climate, Disease, and the End of an Empire — the Roman Climatic Optimum, the Antonine and Cyprianic plagues, and the Justinianic pandemic, read together as one environmental story.

Geoffrey Parker · 2013

Global Crisis: War, Climate Change and Catastrophe in the Seventeenth Century — why states collapsed almost simultaneously from the Ming to the Stuarts, and what the Little Ice Age had to do with it. The clearest case study of climate as a historical force.

Alfred W. Crosby · 1972 / 1986

The Columbian Exchange and Ecological Imperialism — the biological account of 1492 that explains why the exchange was so violently asymmetric.

J. R. McNeill · 2000

Something New Under the Sun: An Environmental History of the Twentieth-Century World — air, water, soils and species in the century when human activity became an environmental force on a planetary scale.

Simon L. Lewis & Mark A. Maslin · 2018

The Human Planet: How We Created the Anthropocene — two scientists on when the Anthropocene should be said to begin, including their case for 1610, the dip in atmospheric CO₂ after conquest and epidemics emptied much of the Americas.

Women & gender

Gerda Lerner · 1986

The Creation of Patriarchy — a classic, now much-debated argument that male dominance was built historically in ancient Mesopotamia, through law, property and the control of women's sexuality, over some two and a half thousand years.

Elizabeth Wayland Barber · 1994

Women's Work: The First 20,000 Years — Women, Cloth, and Society in Early Times — textiles as the great invisible industry of prehistory, largely women's work, reconstructed from spindle whorls, loom weights and surviving cloth.

Merry E. Wiesner-Hanks · 2001; 3rd ed. 2021

Gender in History: Global Perspectives — the standard world-history survey of gender: family, state, religion, work and sexuality, across every continent.

Iris Berger & E. Frances White · 1999

Women in Sub-Saharan Africa: Restoring Women to History — a region-by-region survey that puts women back into African history, from precolonial economies and royal women to colonial rule and independence.

Camilla Townsend · 2006

Malintzin's Choices: An Indian Woman in the Conquest of Mexico — the Nahua woman who interpreted for Cortés, rebuilt from the sources as a person choosing under impossible constraints rather than a traitor or a symbol.

Angela Saini · 2023

The Patriarchs: How Men Came to Rule (US subtitle: The Origins of Inequality) — tests the claim that male domination is natural or universal against archaeology, anthropology and history, and finds it neither.

Slavery, unfree labour & abolition

Orlando Patterson · 1982

Slavery and Social Death: A Comparative Study — slavery compared across dozens of societies, from the ancient Mediterranean to the Caribbean, and defined not as property but as "social death": a person cut off from kin, honour and a past.

Paul E. Lovejoy · 1983

Transformations in Slavery: A History of Slavery in Africa — how slavery inside Africa changed as the Saharan, Red Sea, Indian Ocean and Atlantic trades pulled on it, until colonial-era abolition.

Indrani Chatterjee & Richard M. Eaton, eds. · 2006

Slavery and South Asian History — household, military and agrarian bondage in South Asia: unfreedom that does not fit the Atlantic model at all.

Marcus Rediker · 2007

The Slave Ship: A Human History — the Atlantic crossing itself, seen by captives, sailors and captains: the ship as floating prison and factory.

David Eltis & David Richardson · 2010

Atlas of the Transatlantic Slave Trade — the printed companion to the Slave Voyages database: who was carried from where to where, mapped.

Seymour Drescher · 2009

Abolition: A History of Slavery and Antislavery — how slavery went from normal to outlawed across the world, and why abolition came when and where it did.

Andrés Reséndez · 2016

The Other Slavery: The Uncovered Story of Indian Enslavement in America — the enslavement of Indigenous people across the Americas, which persisted in new forms long after Spain banned it in 1542; Reséndez estimates 2.5 to 5 million people.

Vincent Brown · 2020

Tacky's Revolt: The Story of an Atlantic Slave War — the 1760–61 uprising in Jamaica read as a war fought by enslaved West Africans inside the wider Seven Years' War.

The regions general surveys skip

Africa · François-Xavier Fauvelle · 2013 (English 2018)

The Golden Rhinoceros: Histories of the African Middle Ages — thirty-four vignettes built from the actual surviving evidence for medieval Africa, from Sijilmasa to Mapungubwe.

South Asia · Richard M. Eaton · 2019

India in the Persianate Age, 1000–1765 — India as part of a Persian-speaking world reaching to Iran and Central Asia, rather than a simple Hindu-versus-Muslim story.

Central Asia · Peter B. Golden · 2011

Central Asia in World History — a short, clear history of the steppe and the oasis cities, from the first pastoralists through the Silk Roads and the Mongols to the Soviet era and after.

Southeast Asia · Anthony Reid · 2015

A History of Southeast Asia: Critical Crossroads — the region from its earliest peoples to the present, built around its role as a crossroads of trade, religions and empires.

Australia · Bill Gammage · 2011 · Bruce Pascoe · 2014 · Peter Sutton & Keryn Walshe · 2021

The Biggest Estate on Earth, Dark Emu and Farmers or Hunter-Gatherers? The Dark Emu Debate — on Aboriginal land management, fire-stick farming and the Budj Bim aquaculture systems. Pascoe's claim that pre-contact economies were "agriculture" is disputed by many specialists, and Sutton and Walshe's book is the fullest reply. Read all three: the evidence for skilled, intensive management of Country is not in question — the label is.

The Pacific · Patrick Vinton Kirch · 2000 (rev. ed. 2017)

On the Road of the Winds: An Archaeological History of the Pacific Islands before European Contact — the standard archaeological account of how the Pacific Islands were settled, one of the great maritime expansions in human history.

The Arctic · Robert McGhee · 2004 (US ed. 2005)

The Last Imaginary Place: A Human History of the Arctic World — Dorset, Thule and Norse in the same frame, and Arctic technology treated as the precision engineering it is.

The Arctic · T. Max Friesen & Owen K. Mason, eds. · 2016

The Oxford Handbook of the Prehistoric Arctic — the specialist reference, chapter by chapter, for the peopling of the far north from Siberia to Greenland.

North America · Ned Blackhawk · 2023

The Rediscovery of America: Native Peoples and the Unmaking of U.S. History — the history of the United States retold with Native nations at its centre rather than its margins.

North America · Kathleen DuVal · 2024

Native Nations: A Millennium in North America — a thousand years of Indigenous North America, from the Mississippian cities to Native diplomacy with, and against, European empires.

The Americas · Charles C. Mann · 2005

1491: New Revelations of the Americas Before Columbus — the pre-contact Americas as densely populated, heavily engineered and in no sense a wilderness.

The Caribbean · Carrie Gibson · 2014

Empire's Crossroads: A History of the Caribbean from Columbus to the Present Day — sugar, slavery, revolution and empire across the Spanish, British, French and Dutch islands in one readable volume.

The Andes · Michael E. Moseley · 1992 (rev. ed. 2001)

The Incas and Their Ancestors: The Archaeology of Peru — the Andean past from the first foragers to the Inka, Chavín, Moche, Nasca, Wari and Tiwanaku included.

These shelves map onto Australia, the circumpolar north, North America and the Caribbean in the parallel-civilizations reference.

Read against each other

Five arguments worth hearing from both sides

  • Diamond (1997) and Graeber & Wengrow (2021). Geography and germs as the long-run sorting machine, or people choosing — and un-choosing — how to organize themselves.
  • Harari (2011) and Wragg Sykes (2020). A sudden, sapiens-only "Cognitive Revolution", or symbolic behaviour emerging slowly and patchily, and shared with Neanderthals?
  • Scott (2017) and Podany (2022). The first states as grain-and-tax traps, or as places where scribes, weavers and merchants built lives worth recording.
  • Thornton (1992; 2nd ed. 1998) and Walter Rodney's How Europe Underdeveloped Africa (1972). African rulers and merchants as agents in the Atlantic economy, or a continent systematically drained by it — one of the longest-running arguments in African history.
  • Pascoe (2014) and Sutton & Walshe (2021). Aboriginal Australia as a continent of farmers, or of hunter-gatherers whose management of Country was sophisticated on its own terms.

Data & online resources

Almost every number in this tour — the population hockey stick, the energy mix, life expectancy, GDP per capita, CO₂ — comes from a small set of open, freely browsable datasets. These are where to check a figure, download the series, or dig far past what any single page can hold.

Our World in Data

ourworldindata.org

The tour's most-used source: long-run, open-access charts on population, health, energy, poverty, food, and emissions, each with its data and methods laid bare and downloadable.

The Maddison Project Database

Groningen Growth and Development Centre

The standard long-run reconstruction of GDP per capita by country, stretching back two thousand years — the evidence base behind the "Great Enrichment" and Great-Divergence debates.

HYDE — History Database of the Global Environment

PBL Netherlands Environmental Assessment Agency

Gridded, map-able estimates of population, land use, and agriculture reaching back to 10,000 BCE — the backbone of long-run human-footprint and land-change research.

Seshat: Global History Databank

seshatdatabank.info

A structured, quantitative dataset coding the social complexity — scale, hierarchy, money, writing, infrastructure — of hundreds of past societies from every world region.

Gapminder

gapminder.org

Hans Rosling's project: animated bubble-chart tools and "Dollar Street," built to correct our systematically wrong mental picture of global development.

Smithsonian Human Origins Program

humanorigins.si.edu

Authoritative, regularly updated fossil profiles, interactive timelines, and family-tree resources for the deep-prehistory and Paleolithic chapters.

Museum collections online

British Museum · the Met · Google Arts & Culture

Millions of catalogued objects, many with open-access high-resolution images — the closest thing to walking the galleries of the whole human past from a browser.

Ancient-DNA resources

Reich Lab · Allen Ancient DNA Resource (AADR)

The curated, published ancient-genome datasets that underpin the migration, dispersal, and admixture story running through the early chapters.

UN World Population Prospects

population.un.org

The authoritative modern demographic series — historical estimates from 1950 and projections to 2100 — behind the recent end of the population curve.

Open primary-source archives

The datasets above are syntheses. These are the primary materials themselves — tablets, inscriptions, texts and excavation records — free to read, and the layer a reader who wants to check something rather than take it on trust should go to next.

CDLI — Cuneiform Digital Library Initiative

cdli.earth

Hundreds of thousands of catalogued cuneiform tablets with transliterations and images — the actual administrative records of the first cities in Chapter IV.

Perseus Digital Library

perseus.tufts.edu

Greek and Latin texts with word-by-word morphological and lexical tools, alongside translations — the classical corpus of Chapter V, open and searchable.

Chinese Text Project

ctext.org

The pre-modern Chinese textual tradition — the Analects, the Shiji, the dynastic histories — in parallel original and translation.

Internet History Sourcebooks Project

sourcebooks.fordham.edu

Paul Halsall's long-running collection of public-domain and copy-permitted source texts for teaching, with dedicated African, East Asian, Indian, Islamic, Jewish, Global and Women's sourcebooks alongside the ancient, medieval and modern ones.

Digital Florentine Codex

florentinecodex.getty.edu

The sixteenth-century encyclopedia of Nahua life made by Bernardino de Sahagún with Nahua elders, authors and artists — parallel Nahuatl and Spanish texts and nearly 2,500 painted images, free from the Getty Research Institute.

Pleiades & Trismegistos

pleiades.stoa.org · trismegistos.org

An open gazetteer of ancient places, and a cross-referenced index of papyri and inscriptions — the infrastructure that lets a name in one text be matched to a name in another.

Open Context & the Archaeology Data Service

opencontext.org · archaeologydataservice.ac.uk

Published excavation data — contexts, finds, measurements — rather than only the conclusions drawn from them. This is where a date in a book can be traced back to a trench.

Trans-Atlantic Slave Trade Database

slavevoyages.org

Roughly 36,000 documented voyages assembled from ship manifests and port records — the quantitative backbone for Chapters VII and VIII, and a rare case where the archive of an atrocity was compiled by its perpetrators and can now be read against them. Eltis and Richardson's Atlas, on the slavery shelf, is its printed companion.

Enslaved.org

enslaved.org

Records of individual people — the enslaved, enslavers and others caught up in the historical slave trade — linked across many datasets, with its own academic journal for publishing the underlying datasets. Where the voyages database counts, this one names.
